# Vocabulary

Choose the book icon (**Browse words**) to search Yomiru’s shared dictionary.
Use the first-column star to save an entry and select **Starred** to see your
saved list. Entries can be starred before they appear in an episode. Stars and
practice history stay in the current browser across reloads.
Vocabulary browsing uses English meanings across all subtitle languages.
Study the Japanese words alongside kana, rōmaji, grammatical
category and meanings. In the **Meaning** column, expand **27 more** or a
similar link below the first definition to see the remaining English glosses,
one per bullet. **Frequency** counts occurrences in published subtitles.
The column after the star shows each word's **Mastery** as a whole percentage inside
a progress ring. Words without a score show an em dash.
**Due in** shows how long until the next scheduled review, for example
**9h45m**. Hover over the countdown to see the scheduled date and time.
When the review is due, the word shows **Due now**. An unscheduled word
shows a dash.
**Subcategory** lists the dictionary-backed verb type when available, such as
Godan, Ichidan or Irregular.
The browser shows the most frequent words first by default. Select a column
heading to sort by it, then select it again to reverse the order. **Mastery**
starts with the highest score and keeps words without a score at the end in both
directions. **Due in** starts with the earliest due words and keeps
unscheduled words at the end in both directions. **Japanese** sorts by
reading and **Meaning** by the first gloss.

Filter by category or find a word by Japanese spelling, kana or rōmaji. Search
includes encountered forms with known readings. For example, `desho` finds
`です` when `でしょ` or `でしょう` occurs in published
subtitles. Meanings match from the start of a word, including in expanded
definitions. The browser opens with a lightweight list for sorting. It loads
expanded meanings in pages as you scroll and checks encountered forms when you
enter a search. A spinner appears while results or details load. Sorting by
mastery or next review applies to all matching words.

On desktop, the Meaning column has the most space. On phones,
the table shows Mastery, Due in, Japanese, and the first English
meaning. Personal mastery and review dates stay in your browser.
The vocabulary bar shows the percentage of playable words introduced and
mastered. Gold marks mastered words, violet marks the other introduced words,
and charcoal marks unseen words. Both percentages share the same total.
The library summary exposes both word counts and percentages to screen readers.
Group covers, episode cards, and movie posters show the bar without visible
labels. Hover over or keyboard-focus the
selected title's progress bar to see its percentages. Titles with
reviewed Practice show the same progress in the Library and in the exercise.
Other titles have no progress bar in the library. The global bar counts unique words across
all published reviewed exercises. Group bars use the same rule within the
group, including titles outside the current search or page.

## Practice Japanese meanings

Open **Practice** in a title’s action bar and choose **Word Drill** to begin
a round of dictionary questions. The drill covers starred words first, then
fills the round with frequent words from the published subtitle library.
Choose the English meaning of a kana word or the kana for an English meaning.
Japanese prompts and answer tiles show kanji above kana when available, with
rōmaji below.
On desktop, press **1–4** to answer. The first attempt counts toward the
word’s progress when its reading and meaning match the saved record.
First-try correct answers build a combo and show the saved mastery score.
Choose **Ask Yomiru** beside the combo to ask about the current dictionary
word. The conversation follows you through the round, and each message
keeps its own word attachment. A tutor reply resets the combo and makes that turn unscored.

Open **Practice** (the dumbbell) in the title's action bar and select **Vocabulary**
to study that episode or movie. Copy the link to share it or
open the same title later. Episode links use paths such as
`/neon-genesis-evangelion-tt0112159/s01e01/practice`. Movie links use the
collection and movie slugs. The control is disabled when there is no published
reviewed exercise. The practice menu shows why the mode is unavailable.
Subtitles remain available.

Use **Library** in an episode's Vocabulary exercise to return to the series
gallery with the episode deselected.

Select **Verbs** in the practice menu to study prepared verb forms from
the selected episode or movie. New verbs enter
in frequency order, mixed with forms due for recall. The prompt shows the
dictionary spelling, kana, and rōmaji. In Verb Trainer and Review, the English
line pairs the dictionary gloss with an example of the requested form when
available, as in “to do · am doing”. Select
**?** beside the form label below the English gloss to open a short guide in a
tooltip.
The guide highlights the type of verb you’re practising in the first row and
gives the rules in rōmaji. The same help appears on conjugation questions in
Review. Choose the requested form from four cards with Japanese spelling, kana,
and rōmaji. If the spelling
matches the kana, the card shows it once above the rōmaji. Each form has its
own mastery and review date. Episode Practice credits the verb’s meaning when
you recognize it in an inflected subtitle.

**Review** mixes meanings for words you’ve been introduced to with conjugation
forms you’ve answered in this browser. Due questions come first. Choose **Review again** after any round for extra practice. Practicing
before a review is due keeps its scheduled date and spaced-repetition credit.

A meaning question highlights the form used in a reviewed subtitle phrase and
shows four English choices. Choose the meaning of the highlighted word. Your first
selection counts and a miss lets you try the same question again. The
English subtitle stays hidden. The toolbar shows the source movie or series
and episode, with the subtitle’s start time when available. Review searches
all published titles with reviewed Practice questions. It prefers a different
title that uses the same meaning, then a different cue in the same title. Words without a
matching reviewed phrase stay out of the queue. When an older answered record lacks
a contextual meaning, Review uses the saved dictionary meaning.
Review shows how many questions you completed and lets you know when no questions
match your practice history.

The full-width toolbar centers the source title. Series show the episode code
and title below the series name. Movies show the film title. On narrow screens,
the source timestamp appears on its own line when available. In both Review and
Verb Trainer, the round count appears at the right, such as “1 of 12”. The
vocabulary bar spans the toolbar below the titles and gives its percentages to
screen readers. Review uses library-wide vocabulary progress.

On mobile, hold your device upright. Review opens in fullscreen when the
browser supports it. If you turn the device sideways, rotate it back to resume
your current question. Use **Library** to return to the previous display mode.

When subtitle timing is available, the timeline below the vocabulary bar shows
the current subtitle time and total duration. Click or drag the scrubber to
jump to the closest subtitle question. Use the arrow keys to move between
subtitles or Home and End to jump to the first or last one. Your mastery and
pending retries stay intact as you move around the episode.

The Yomiru fox reveals a pair of subtitles while practice loads. With reduced
motion enabled, the fox and subtitles stay still. Read the large mixed kana,
with rōmaji and the full English translation below. When source spelling is
available, it appears in a small gray line above the kana. If the highlight is
in Japanese, choose the English meaning. If the highlight is in English, choose
the Japanese word from cards with spelling, kana, and rōmaji, as in Verb Trainer.
In a regular quiz, both subtitles stay visible. Practice randomly chooses a
direction and shows four choices. Japanese choices use the exact forms in the current subtitle.
When the English highlight is ambiguous or the line has fewer than three
plausible Japanese distractors, the question uses English choices instead.
Practice prefers related English distractors. For colors, it looks for other
hues in the episode first, then in the rest of the published library.
Each phrase starts at its first eligible word and continues left to right,
including repeated words. A reviewed particle can be a target when it has a
clear contextual answer and three reviewed alternatives. Particle questions
highlight Japanese and offer English choices. Finish each phrase before the
quiz advances to the next subtitle.

Episode Practice quizzes only prepared occurrences. A
Japanese-to-English verb question can highlight a complete inflected form such
as 分かりません. The answer is its meaning in that line, and the review belongs to
the existing dictionary entry 分かる. The episode's Practice total counts the distinct
words that can be answered in that sequence.

When Practice introduces a word, **New word** appears above one button with its
meaning in the subtitle. On desktop, the button is numbered `1`. Click or tap
the meaning, or press `1`, to continue through the line. At the end of a line
that introduced a word, Practice jumps back to the first word and quizzes the
whole line. Answer each quiz question correctly to move to the next subtitle.
Acknowledging the new word queues the replay.
Only quiz answers contribute to mastery and combo.
Words you previously saw or missed can still get a teaching card. Practice
remembers when you tap a word's meaning or answer it correctly.

A first-try correct answer briefly shows the credited vocabulary entry's
dictionary spelling, kana, English meaning and current mastery. Conjugation
questions show the verb form that earned the score and its label. Whenever an
answer changes saved mastery, the notification shows both percentages and
animates the bar between them when motion is enabled.

On desktop, press `1`–`4` to choose an answer. The answer buttons sit at the
bottom of the screen, with the phrase centered above them.
**Library**, **Practice** and **Combo** share one row. Answers form a two-by-two
grid on narrow screens. Short screens use tighter spacing and can scroll if
needed. Use **Library** to leave practice.

A correct answer turns the chosen tile green and plays a brief chime. A miss
turns only the chosen tile red, resets the combo and lets you try the same
word again. Only the first attempt can earn initial mastery credit. First-try correct answers
without a dictionary peek add to your combo. The fox breathes between answers,
winks on regular successes, does a double hop at five, dances at ten and wears
a crown from fifteen until the streak resets. The counter flashes gold every
five answers in a row. With reduced motion, the fox stays still and still wears
the crown for an active streak of fifteen or more.

In regular Japanese-to-English questions, hover over a word with a dotted underline to
highlight it in both scripts.
Click or tap either spelling to open a tooltip by the matching rōmaji word.
The top row uses middle dots between the dictionary kanji spelling, when
available, and the cue's kana and rōmaji. Under the English gloss, the tooltip
shows the part of speech and, for verbs, the conjugation class when known.
Your mastery percentage or “Not practiced” sits on the right. A particle
shows its grammar label and any saved mastery.
The quiz also offers lookups for names whose spelling and reading match JMdict.
The highlighted Japanese quiz word stays locked. Peeking resets your combo, and
a correct answer on that question leaves the combo at zero. When a line is
queued for replay, its Japanese-to-English questions hide the translation
and disable word lookups through the end of the replay. English-to-Japanese
questions still show the English prompt and have word lookups off.

Both directions use the same mastery and review schedule. If a word is due for
review, Practice disables lookups the first time it asks about that word during
the session. Your first choice counts toward the review, and a correct answer
advances in subtitle order.

Accepted tile taps give a brief vibration in browsers that support the Vibration API,
such as Chrome for Android, when device vibration is enabled. Safari on iPhone
and iPad lacks support for the Vibration API.

Reviewed particle questions earn mastery and can appear in Review. Browse
words includes particles and their dictionary meanings. Particle occurrences
without a reviewed question remain available for lookup where aligned.

Practice loads cards for upcoming rounds as you play. The bar covers all
words in the reviewed exercise, including words that have yet to load.
A word counts as introduced as soon as Practice shows it, even if you leave
it unanswered.
Words need a usable reading and English meaning to appear in the game. A
shared word counts once and carries the same progress across title selections
and practice games.
The browser saves reviews automatically as you play. A first correct quiz
answer gives the word a starting score of about 9%. The score estimates
whether you'll remember the meaning seven days from now without more
practice. The estimate uses a half-life model with fixed starting
assumptions. It has yet to be calibrated on Yomiru learners.
Regular quiz appearances, including in other episodes, leave the next review
date in place. At the scheduled check, a correct first answer after at least a day
can refresh the current estimate. A gap long enough for the review can also
slow the word's decay. Progress to the next mastery stage requires its full
gap since the most recent quiz appearance. A miss on a scheduled review lowers the score
and brings the next review closer. Mastered words have passed
the final review stage, which includes a successful check after at least
30 days. Older multiple-choice history informs the starting estimate.
Mastery milestones come from scheduled reviews. Progress is stored in
the current site and browser profile.

The matching game remains available in code. To make it the global default,
set `defaultPracticeGame` to `"matching"` in
[`VocabularyPractice.jsx`](../web/src/VocabularyPractice.jsx).

For local development with the current published catalogue, run
`YOMIRU_PUBLIC_CATALOGUE=1 npm run dev -- --port auto` from `web/`. With no
titles checked, the preview builds the vocabulary selection from the titles
currently listed in the public catalogue. The preview publishes no changes.

### Run contextual quizzes locally

Import the [private subtitle corpus](publishing.md#regenerate-tracks-from-postgresql)
into local PostgreSQL. Start the preview from `web/` with
`YOMIRU_PUBLIC_CATALOGUE=1 YOMIRU_PRACTICE_CONTEXT_SOCKET=/path/to/socket npm run dev -- --port auto`.
The preview uses the public catalogue for word selection and loads examples
from the local database. Hosted serving uses the same context tables when
they are populated and `YOMIRU_PRACTICE_CONTEXT=1` is set.
Rebuild and reimport older context projections to include every aligned cue
with playable words. Existing projections can serve the retained cues and spans
until refreshed. The hosted app uses stored English from the active subtitle
revision, with a fallback to the active English ASS track when it has a single
Japanese cue and a single English line at matching start and end times.
The preview reads peek meanings from the current registry in the local
database. Examples can still display when the local database has only the
context tables. Hosted episode practice can recover missing peeks from the
archive for the exact same subtitle cue and displayed readings.
Aligned compounds get a single peek across their full kana and rōmaji. If
the shared dictionary has no English gloss, reviewed episode Practice can use
the gloss saved with that episode.

### Ask Yomiru

In episode or movie Practice, select the toolbar fox to discuss the current
subtitle. Choose **Ask about this word** in a word lookup to discuss that word
in context. The subtitle is attached to your first message. Yomiru's avatar and
a three-dot bubble appear while he prepares a reply. Use **Previous line**
in chat to go back one subtitle. Closing the chat resumes Practice. A quiz turn
where you receive help through chat earns no mastery or combo credit.

The chat sends your question, recent messages and the available subtitle context
to the configured model when you select Send. Yomiru can explain the Japanese,
give examples and check the published English. His replies teach for listening
comprehension and lead with rōmaji, with kana and kanji as reading and spelling
references when useful. The conversation lives in the open Practice session.
Model replies do not revise published subtitles.

Local Vite development uses a running AgentBridge on `127.0.0.1:8082`.
Set `YOMIRU_AGENTBRIDGE_PORT` if the local instance uses another port. Requests
use `codex/gpt-6.2-sol` at low reasoning with AgentBridge logging disabled for
the conversation. Start the preview with the command above.

The hosted route uses OpenRouter. Configure `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` and
`YOMIRU_OPENROUTER_CHAT_MODEL` in the server runtime through approved
provisioning. The selected model must have a provider that supports the
data-collection restriction. Gemini 4 Hydrogen uses low reasoning and a
1,536-token generation limit to leave room for the visible reply. The API returns
unavailable until both settings are present. The app's short request quota is per
instance. A public edge rate limit for `POST /api/practice-chat` can apply a quota
across instances.

## What counts as a word

Analysis uses pinned UniDic-lite dictionary forms, lexical readings
and grammatical roles. Inflected forms share a vocabulary ID; words with the
same pronunciation are not merged merely because they sound alike. The
vocabulary catalogue includes particles, auxiliaries, Japanese number words and
counters. Practice uses the occurrences of words eligible for practice. Vocabulary analysis
excludes names, title-specific named entities, raw vocalizations, punctuation,
source speaker and ruby annotations, digit strings and single Latin-letter
tokens. Conventional interjections, mimetic words and ordinary uses of a
spelling shared with a name remain eligible. Compounds follow the analyser's
segmentation rather than a hand-curated expression list. Readings and segmentation are automatic,
not independently verified pronunciation or word-sense annotation.

The shared master registry owns each lexeme's canonical dictionary headword,
reading, English meanings, resolution status and dictionary provenance.
Episode vocabulary assets contain IDs, occurrence counts, encountered forms,
additional readings for those forms and source examples. Every episode using a
word receives the same meanings. Adding an episode reuses resolved entries.
Meaning changes create a new registry revision without rewriting episode assets
or Japanese subtitle sources. The browser and export pin that registry revision
while loading. Master coverage grows with the published subtitle library.

Each episode has one versioned Japanese anchor for vocabulary. All newly
published translations reference that anchor. Changing target language, reading
mode or translated text does not change its Japanese word set. Publication
rejects a different Japanese source for an anchored episode; changing the source
requires an explicit complete source migration rather than silently mixing it
with existing translations. Existing legacy subtitle variants remain available;
vocabulary is prepared from an administrator-selected exact published generation.

A reviewed Practice source may include additional cues from the same original
Japanese captions. Bind that review to the active vocabulary revision and an
existing English track. Keep its paired cue data separate from the immutable
Stremio file and preserve the published vocabulary and track records.

## CSV API fields

The vocabulary API's CSV response uses UTF-8 with a byte-order mark, a header
and one row per vocabulary ID.
Fields contain the stable ID, Japanese dictionary form, kana, rōmaji, English
meanings (`meaning_eng`), encountered forms, Japanese part of speech, occurrence
count, source episodes, an example sentence with episode and timestamp, meaning
status, dictionary attribution/license/version and Japanese source revisions.
Commas, quotes and newlines are escaped, and spreadsheet formula prefixes are
neutralized. Retain attribution when sharing derived files.

Meanings are dictionary definitions matching the word's reading and broad
grammatical role; they are not selected for the scene. Missing meanings remain
blank with an explicit status and visible missing count, and never remove words.
English definitions use JMdict. The master registry also supports editorial
corrections. A genuine lexical entry with no supported English meaning stays
in the catalogue. Matching practice requires a usable reading and meaning.
Editorial corrections take precedence and retain separate provenance.

For future Anki use, keep `vocabulary_id` as the first field of a custom note type,
then map Japanese, reading and meaning to separate fields. Keep the IDs when
reimporting to update notes. For CSV import setup, see the
[Anki text import guide](https://docs.ankiweb.net/manual/importing/text-files).
Automatic deck creation, Anki synchronization, audio and images are future work.

## Administrator preparation

After the incremental catalogue is live, seed its shared dictionary with
the English JMdict Simplified archive through the reviewed publication route.

```sh
uv run yomiru jmdict-rows --dictionary /path/to/jmdict-eng.json.tgz |
  node web/scripts/seed-jmdict.mjs --executor EXECUTOR --deployment HOSTNAME
```

The import records original JMdict entry IDs. Matching spelling, reading and
part of speech can link existing words and fill blank meanings. Existing
definitions and IDs take precedence. Study counts only include words used by
the selected subtitles.

Run the same import after deploying support for Verb Trainer to backfill the
existing dictionary. It uses JMdict’s verb classes to prepare written forms,
kana, rōmaji, and three distinct distractors for each question. Repeating the
import refreshes changed conjugation data without replacing existing
definitions. The trainer uses published verb occurrences with an unambiguous
match and supported forms. Historical classes and unresolved variants stay out
of the drill.

After verifying the production executor and AgentBridge, install the macOS
review service on the AgentBridge host.

```sh
uv run yomiru review-service install --node /absolute/path/to/node \
  --executor /absolute/path/to/executor --deployment HOSTNAME
```

The service runs a bounded batch at startup and every 15 minutes. The same flags
identify the service to `review-service uninstall`. To process one batch manually,
run `node web/scripts/review-words.mjs --executor EXECUTOR --deployment HOSTNAME`.
The reviewer receives excerpted evidence and dictionary candidates. The server
verifies the cited cue and entry before updating a meaning. Unsupported
decisions remain unresolved. For a flagged tokenization error, prepare the
revised analysis and matching vocabulary for the same archived source, then
pass `--correct-word-id WORD_ID` to the incremental uploader. It verifies
the replacement counts against the archive and preserves the subtitle tracks.

Generation and dictionary lookup run locally before publication. Publication
validates the master registry and episode references, then imports them into
Neon. Public serving counts distinct word IDs for the selection and loads
study and export details on demand. Public requests perform no tokenization or
inference.

Obtain current JMdict English data from the
[JMdict project](https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/JMdict-EDICT_Dictionary_Project),
or its [JMdict Simplified distribution](https://github.com/scriptin/jmdict-simplified).
The loader accepts JMdict XML, gzip XML, or the distribution's English
`.json.tgz` archive, reading without archive extraction. Keep the dictionary
outside tracked source; preserve its checksum with reconstruction inputs.

Build or refresh the master registry across a complete published library:

```sh
uv run yomiru vocabulary-registry --data-dir /path/to/library \
  --pairs-root /path/to/exact-generation-pairs \
  --dictionary /path/to/jmdict-eng.json.tgz
```

This requires exact canonical Japanese generation inputs for every covered
episode, preserves Japanese anchors and ASS assets, and resolves meanings once
across the registry. The analysis version is explicit. Version 2 fixes analyzer
token invalidation during romanization and uses lexical headwords/readings;
invalid historical entries are replaced rather than assigned invented meanings.
Version 3 excludes digit strings and single Latin letters. Rebuild and publish
the complete library to apply version 3 to existing episodes.
Older assets support rōmaji search for kana forms immediately. Rebuild and
publish the vocabulary assets to add readings for their kanji forms.

Version 4 excludes proper names and raw vocalizations during analysis. For the
incremental catalogue, stage the cleanup against the exact archived Japanese
source and the current vocabulary descriptor for each title:

```sh
uv run yomiru study-vocabulary --pairs PAIRS.json --japanese-source ORIGINAL.srt \
  --data-dir STAGING --video-id VIDEO_ID --exclude-spans REVIEWED.json
```

The optional review file maps cue IDs to arrays of `[start, end]` token spans
using zero-based Unicode code-point offsets in the cleaned Japanese cue. Apply
it only to that cue's reviewed use. The command emits the new vocabulary
descriptor and private archive manifest. Combine those records into a JSON
array, adding the current vocabulary hash as `previousVocabulary` to each.
Use one staging directory for all affected titles and put the plan there.
For a new title, pass the same review file to `yomiru publish --exclude-spans`
along with the original Japanese source and private archive directory.

```sh
node web/scripts/upload-episode.mjs --study-batch STAGING/plan.json \
  --executor EXECUTOR --deployment HOSTNAME
```

The batch activation checks the original source, archived token counts, current
vocabulary revision and unchanged IDs for the surviving words. It publishes
the replacements together without re-uploading or changing any subtitle track.
An unchanged Japanese source keeps its reviewed vocabulary when a translation
is added.

To enrich an exact already-published episode, without altering its ASS assets:

```sh
uv run yomiru vocabulary --pairs /path/to/pairs.json --video-id tt1234567:1:1 \
  --dictionary /path/to/jmdict-eng.json.tgz --data-dir /path/to/library
```

The pairs must match that episode's published generation checksum. New episodes
use the same `--dictionary` option with `yomiru publish`. Without a dictionary,
already-resolved master entries retain their meanings; only genuinely new entries
remain unresolved. Dictionary refreshes keep lexical IDs and Japanese anchors
unchanged. Refresh dictionary data at least monthly and publish a complete snapshot.

Correct meanings centrally with `yomiru vocabulary-meanings --data-dir
/path/to/library --corrections /path/to/corrections.json`. The JSON has an `entries`
array. Each item supplies an existing `id`, `language` set to `eng`, a nonempty
`meanings` array and public HTTPS `sourceUrl`. Corrections are labelled curated,
retain evidence, and survive dictionary refresh. No episode asset changes.
Use `--update-registry` with the administrator upload command when intentionally
publishing changed meanings. Ordinary episode uploads preserve current resolved
meanings and add new entries without reverting prior corrections.

Use the existing [upload workflow](publishing.md#upload-and-serve), first with
`--dry-run`. Vocabulary assets are immutable and content-addressed alongside
subtitle assets. The snapshot binds each movie and episode to its vocabulary
hash, anchor and exact count. The upload script requires matching vocabulary
for each new or revised subtitle track, and preserves existing coverage.
Referenced vocabulary objects are validated before activation. The
compressed master registry is retained as a content-addressed import and
restore asset. It has a 3 MiB upload limit and a 16 MiB decoded limit. The
vocabulary API reads the imported rows.
Subtitle updates reuse already-published vocabulary when the exact Japanese
source remains present. New movies and episodes ship with their vocabulary in
the same publication.
